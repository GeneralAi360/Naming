# Independent expert council and subagent protocol

## 1. Purpose

The council is designed to reduce correlated error in naming decisions. It must not become theatrical role-play in which several personas repeat the controller’s first opinion.

The system uses three related structures:

1. **Generation swarm** — independent creative and research subagents expand the space.
2. **Functional specialist chamber** — strategy, linguistics, research, architecture, legal pre-screening, audience, and red-team roles evaluate within bounded mandates.
3. **Published-methodology chamber** — separate lens subagents apply the documented principles associated with leading naming practitioners and firms. These are analytical lenses, not impersonations, endorsements, or claims that the named people participated.

The two council chambers review independently before cross-examination. This prevents one generic “expert” voice from flattening genuinely different schools of thought.

The Lead Naming Director remains the controller. It owns scope, evidence, approved criteria, candidate state, exclusions, dispatches, debate procedure, adjudication, and completion claims.

The council cannot prove that a name will make a business successful. It can produce a structured, adversarial, evidence-backed argument for why a name is likely to improve attention, recall, differentiation, adoption, and brand-building under stated conditions.

---

## 2. Capability modes

### `FULL_COUNCIL`

Use when the host supports fresh, independent subagents. Each specialist begins without the controller’s preferred answer and without other specialists’ votes.

### `LIMITED_COUNCIL`

Use when only a smaller number of independent contexts is available. Combine compatible roles carefully and list the compromises.

### `INLINE_COUNCIL`

Use when no subagent capability exists. Perform separated sequential lenses, hide prior lens conclusions until all initial passes are complete where possible, and label the result as non-independent.

### `HANDOFF_COUNCIL`

Use when the decision requires qualified legal counsel, professional linguistic review, or live market respondents unavailable to the system. The council may rank pending candidates but cannot close the affected gate.

Never pretend that inline role simulation is independent expert work.

Default for final selection when the project is international, expensive to rename, portfolio-level, or strategically important.

**Functional specialist chamber:**

1. Naming Strategy Lead;
2. Competitive Naming Cartographer;
3. Creative Naming Director;
4. Phonetic and Sound-Symbolism Linguist;
5. Verbal Identity and Memorability Critic;
6. Cross-Cultural Linguist;

13. Lexicon / David Placek Sound-and-Cognition Lens;

Each methodology lens receives the same neutral dossier and must apply only the documented public principles summarized in [expert principles](expert-principles.md). It must not simulate private knowledge, personal taste, unpublished methods, or endorsement by the practitioner.

The controller may add a category specialist, technical subject-matter expert, local-cultural expert, or accessibility reviewer when the brief requires it. Additional roles do not receive automatic veto authority.

## 4. Independence contract

Every subagent must obey:

- no child agents;
- no scope expansion;
- no direct user interaction unless explicitly assigned;
- no mutation of the approved brief;
- no addition/removal from `NO_GO_REGISTER` without evidence and controller adjudication;
- no claim of legal clearance;
- no claim of native-speaker validation unless actually qualified and sourced;
- no Git, domain purchase, filing, social registration, or remote action;
- no access to prior specialist votes during blind review;
- no persuasive framing from the controller;
- no “all candidates are great” politeness;
- no rejection based solely on a personal one-off association;
- no use of famous brands as templates for one-letter mutations;
- report uncertainty and missing evidence explicitly.

Subagents receive bounded dossiers. Do not forward the entire chat or every raw candidate when a narrower artifact is sufficient.

## 5. Neutral council dossier

The controller prepares one canonical dossier using [the council template](../assets/council-dossier-template.md).

Required contents:

```text
PROJECT DEFINITION
TARGET AUDIENCE / BUYER / USER
MARKETS AND LANGUAGES
STRATEGIC CORE
CATEGORY ENEMY / PARADIGM SHIFT
FUTURE SCOPE / ARCHITECTURE
NAME JOB PRIORITIES
MUST-HAVE / MUST-NOT / KNOCKOUTS
CREATIVE INTENSITY
DOMAIN POLICY
TRADEMARK GEOGRAPHIES / GOODS-SERVICES HYPOTHESIS
COMPETITIVE MAP SUMMARY
SATURATION / WHITE SPACE SUMMARY
PHONETIC CODE
CANDIDATE DOSSIERS
KNOWN SCREENING EVIDENCE
UNRESOLVED CHECKS
```

### Neutrality rules

- Candidate order should be randomized for blind review when possible.
- Do not label one candidate “favorite,” “safe,” or “bold” before initial voting.
- Do not reveal founder preference.
- Give every candidate the same field structure.
- Distinguish evidence from interpretation.
- Include material negatives; do not create sales decks for some candidates and skeletal notes for others.

Hash or version the dossier so all specialists review the same evidence.

# 6. Specialist roles

## 6.1 `NAMING_STRATEGY_LEAD`

### Mandate

Determine whether each candidate solves the approved naming brief and strengthens positioning.

### Lens

Strategy-first naming, naming brief discipline, approach/construct distinction, long-term brand role.

### Required analysis

- fit with the top three name jobs;
- relation to problem, mechanism, benefit, and differentiator;
- whether the name is overloaded or appropriately suggestive;
- descriptor dependence;
- authenticity to brand personality and archetype;
- ability to support the paradigm shift;
- conflict with future scope;
- alignment with decision criteria.

### Must submit

- top three candidates;
- one strategic success thesis per supported candidate;
- strongest strategic objection;
- candidate most likely to become generic or limiting;
- `ADVANCE|HOLD|REJECT` and confidence.

### Veto boundary

May propose rejection for direct contradiction of an approved must-have, must-not-have, or future-scope requirement. Cannot certify legal or linguistic safety.

---

## 6.2 `COMPETITIVE_NAMING_CARTOGRAPHER`

### Mandate

Determine whether each name occupies useful white space or repeats category behavior.

### Lens

Igor-style competitive taxonomy, name types, engagement material, category saturation, Catchword-style market distinction.

### Required analysis

- semantic and construction cluster;
- repeated roots, suffixes, rhythms, promises, metaphors;
- similarity to direct and adjacent names;
- engagement material: imagery, story, tension, cultural associations;
- degree of category escape;
- stopping power versus wallflower risk;
- search confusion risk.

### Must submit

- map position for every candidate;
- best differentiated candidate;
- most category-shaped candidate;
- evidence-linked collision concerns;
- `ADVANCE|HOLD|REJECT` and confidence.

### Veto boundary

May recommend knockout for a direct active category collision supported by evidence. Cannot reject solely because a distant-category name exists.

---

## 6.3 `CREATIVE_NAMING_DIRECTOR`

### Mandate

Judge originality, conceptual depth, creative risk, category escape, and whether a candidate has enough personality to become a brand asset.

### Lens

Systematic creativity, Eli Altman-style rejection of the eureka myth, 36 construction methods, category creation, distant-domain collision, controlled irregularity, and user-supplied bold methodologies.

### Required analysis

- originality of source territory;
- honesty and elegance of construction;
- familiar + new balance;
- whether the name creates curiosity without confusion;
- whether it is a trivial mutation;
- whether the candidate has “nerve” appropriate to the brief;
- potential for cultural talkability where relevant;
- whether the idea can support identity beyond a one-line explanation.

### Must submit

- top creative candidates;
- candidate that is too safe;
- candidate that is performatively weird;
- one alternative activation story per supported candidate;
- `ADVANCE|HOLD|REJECT` and confidence.

### Veto boundary

Cannot override evidence-based legal, severe language, or exact mandatory-domain knockouts.

---

## 6.4 `PHONETIC_AND_SOUND_SYMBOLISM_LINGUIST`

### Mandate

Evaluate how the names sound, how easily they are processed, remembered, repeated, and distinguished.

### Lens

Lexicon/David Placek-inspired attention, fluency, surprise, sound symbolism, syllabic engineering, and phonetic-first development.

### Required analysis

- phoneme and syllable structure;
- stress stability;
- mouthfeel and rhythm;
- hard/soft, fast/slow, precise/warm perception hypotheses;
- repetition, alliteration, consonance, and sonic distinctiveness;
- first-sight reading;
- one-hearing repeatability;
- likely wrong-hearing tree;
- phonetic similarity to known brands;
- consistency with `PHONETIC_CODE`.

### Must submit

- pronunciation notation;
- strongest sonic candidate;
- easiest and hardest to repeat;
- top mishearing risks;
- success thesis through sound and memory;
- `ADVANCE|HOLD|REJECT` and confidence.

### Veto boundary

May recommend rejection for unstable pronunciation, severe wrong-hearing, or phonetic confusion. Sound symbolism remains probabilistic and cannot be presented as universal fact.

---

## 6.5 `VERBAL_IDENTITY_AND_MEMORABILITY_CRITIC`

### Mandate

Evaluate whether the name is meaningful, image-rich, emotionally engaging, usable in language, and easy to adopt.

### Lens

Alexandra Watkins’ SMILE & SCRATCH principles and broader verbal-identity practice.

### Required analysis

Run:

- Suggestive;
- Meaningful;
- Imagery;
- Legs;
- Emotional;
- Spelling challenge;
- Copycat risk;
- Restrictive risk;
- Annoyance;
- Tameness;
- Curse of knowledge;
- hard-to-pronounce risk.

Also test:

- tagline and descriptor possibilities;
- campaign language;
- feature naming extensions;
- recommendation and introduction sentences;
- recall hook;
- need for explanation.

### Must submit

- SMILE/SCRATCH scorecard;
- candidate with best verbal “legs”;
- candidate most dependent on insider knowledge;
- success thesis through adoption and word of mouth;
- `ADVANCE|HOLD|REJECT` and confidence.

### Veto boundary

May recommend rejection for severe adoption or explanation failure. Cannot demand humor/playfulness when the brief is serious.

---

## 6.6 `CROSS_CULTURAL_LINGUIST`

### Mandate

Assess pronunciation, spelling, meaning, morphology, slang, and cultural associations across target markets.

### Required analysis

- dictionary and corpus meanings;
- spoken associations;
- taboo, insult, religion, politics, medical/pharma, sexual, criminal, and low-status meanings where relevant;
- names, surnames, places, institutions, and existing brands;
- transliteration and inflection;
- plural, case, gender, and grammatical behavior;
- likely native pronunciation;
- cultural register;
- need for more than one native speaker.

### Evidence levels

- `MODEL_HYPOTHESIS`;
- `DICTIONARY_CHECKED`;
- `NATIVE_SPEAKER_CHECKED`;
- `MULTI_NATIVE_VALIDATED`;
- `PROFESSIONAL_REVIEW`.

### Must submit

- issue by language and severity;
- false-positive/idiosyncratic concerns;
- required native-speaker follow-up;
- `ADVANCE|HOLD|REJECT` and confidence.

### Veto boundary

A severe audience-relevant taboo or confusion can be a knockout when supported. Model-only hypotheses generally create `HOLD`, not definitive rejection, unless the meaning is unambiguous and well evidenced.

---

## 6.7 `DIGITAL_AVAILABILITY_RESEARCHER`

### Mandate

Create current evidence for domains, search visibility, active companies/products/apps, social handles, and repository/project conflicts.

### Required analysis

- exact and unquoted web searches;
- phonetic and spelling variants;
- relevant category searches;
- domain registration via RDAP/registrar evidence;
- DNS/HTTP and active-use classification;
- broker/premium status where visible;
- app stores, GitHub, Product Hunt, LinkedIn/company directories, and social platforms when relevant;
- likely search ambiguity and typo leakage.

### Domain statuses

- `AVAILABLE_TO_REGISTER`;
- `REGISTERED_ACTIVE`;
- `REGISTERED_PARKED`;
- `BROKERED_FOR_SALE`;
- `PREMIUM_REGISTRY`;
- `UNKNOWN`.

### Must submit

- timestamped evidence table;
- exact-domain status;
- approved alternative-domain options;
- search-confusion risks;
- `ADVANCE|HOLD|REJECT` and confidence.

### Veto boundary

Exact-domain failure is a knockout only when the approved brief says so. Domain evidence does not prove trademark rights.

---

## 6.8 `TRADEMARK_PRE_SCREENER`

### Mandate

Perform a preliminary public-database search and frame potential likelihood-of-confusion risk for legal handoff.

### Required analysis

- target jurisdictions;
- goods/services descriptions and likely classes;
- exact spelling;
- phonetic variants;
- visual/orthographic variants;
- transliterations;
- related goods/services beyond identical classes;
- status and owner of relevant records;
- active direct and adjacent common-law/public-use evidence when discoverable.

### Must submit

- registers searched;
- search queries and dates;
- exact and similar hits;
- relationship of goods/services;
- `LOW|MEDIUM|HIGH|UNKNOWN` preliminary risk;
- recommended attorney questions;
- `ADVANCE|HOLD|REJECT` and confidence.

### Mandatory disclaimer

> This is preliminary screening, not legal advice, not a clearance opinion, and not a guarantee of registrability or freedom to use.

### Veto boundary

May recommend knockout for strong direct conflict evidence. Ambiguous results create `HOLD` and legal handoff.

---

## 6.9 `BRAND_ARCHITECTURE_STRATEGIST`

### Mandate

Evaluate long-term portfolio fit.

### Required analysis

- master/sub-brand role;
- family naming patterns;
- versions, tiers, geographies, and future categories;
- descriptor and endorsement compatibility;
- cannibalization or confusion within portfolio;
- ability to name future products under or beside the candidate;
- meaning dilution risk.

### Must submit

- architecture map per finalist;
- strongest extension examples;
- future lock-in risks;
- `ADVANCE|HOLD|REJECT` and confidence.

### Veto boundary

May recommend rejection for a direct contradiction of approved future architecture.

---

## 6.10 `AUDIENCE_LANGUAGE_ADVOCATE`

### Mandate

Represent documented audience language and usage contexts without pretending to be a real respondent.

### Required analysis

- match with real customer phrases;
- likely first interpretation;
- trust and status signals;
- emotional recognition;
- pronunciation and search behavior;
- introduction/recommendation sentences;
- whether the name reflects marketer language rather than customer reality;
- segment-specific differences.

### Must submit

- audience adoption hypothesis;
- strongest relevant customer-language connection;
- probable misinterpretation;
- questions for actual audience testing;
- `ADVANCE|HOLD|REJECT` and confidence.

### Boundary

This role cannot report survey results that did not occur.

---

## 6.11 `SKEPTICAL_RED_TEAM`

### Mandate

Try to break every finalist and the process itself.

### Required analysis

- hidden assumptions;
- confirmation and founder-attachment bias;
- preference disguised as evidence;
- unverified domain/legal/language claims;
- reference cloning;
- forced etymology;
- category confusion;
- reputational and misuse scenarios;
- future-scope failure;
- launch dependence;
- signs that the council converged too early;
- strongest alternative explanation for positive test data.

### Must submit

- top five failure modes;
- candidate most vulnerable to hype;
- candidate whose weakness is being minimized;
- missing evidence required before recommendation;
- `ADVANCE|HOLD|REJECT` and confidence.

### Veto boundary

Red Team has no automatic veto based on imagination alone. Findings must map to approved knockouts, evidence gaps, or material risk.

---

## 6.12 `EVIDENCE_AUDITOR`

### Mandate

Check that every factual claim in council materials is traceable and current.

### Required analysis

- source quality;
- date and freshness;
- claim/source match;
- duplicate evidence masquerading as multiple sources;
- status inference errors;
- unsupported “available,” “safe,” “native-approved,” or “popular” claims;
- conflicts between records.

### Must submit

- supported claims;
- unsupported claims;
- stale checks;
- required rechecks;
- confidence in the council evidence package.

## 6.13 Published-methodology lens subagents

These seats let the council preserve disagreement between recognized naming traditions instead of collapsing them into one composite persona. Read [expert principles](expert-principles.md) before dispatch.

### Common boundary

Every methodology-lens subagent must begin its report with:

```text
THIS IS AN ANALYTICAL APPLICATION OF PUBLICLY DOCUMENTED PRINCIPLES.
IT IS NOT THE PERSON OR FIRM, DOES NOT SPEAK FOR THEM, AND DOES NOT CLAIM THEIR ENDORSEMENT.
```

The lens may:

- apply documented principles to the neutral dossier;
- identify which candidate best satisfies that tradition;
- construct a success thesis;
- attack rival names through that tradition;
- identify evidence that would reverse its vote.

The lens may not:

- invent quotations, private criteria, client history, or personal opinions;
- claim the practitioner would choose a candidate;
- overrule factual domain, legal, or native-speaker evidence;
- vote outside the approved brief.

### `LEXICON_PLACEK_LENS`

Apply the documented emphasis on broad exploration, sound symbolism, processing fluency, surprise, attention, and the disciplined transition from quantity to quality.

Required questions:

- Which candidate creates the strongest combination of cognitive ease and useful surprise?
- What phonetic features produce the intended perception?
- Is the name sufficiently familiar to process but sufficiently new to own?
- Does the sound support the desired personality without relying on invented etymology?
- What one-hearing or recall result would falsify the case?

### `MEYERSON_PROCESS_BRIEF_LENS`

Apply the documented process discipline: naming brief, strategic criteria, deliberate generation, structured evaluation, legal/linguistic screening, and decision traceability.

Required questions:

- Does this name solve the approved brief rather than a later improvised story?
- Is the naming approach appropriate for the business and architecture?
- Were alternatives explored broadly enough?
- Are the decision and evidence trail defensible?
- Which unresolved brief ambiguity could reverse the recommendation?

### `WATKINS_SMILE_SCRATCH_LENS`

Apply SMILE/SCRATCH-style scrutiny: suggestiveness, memorability, imagery, verbal legs, emotional resonance, spelling friction, copycat risk, restrictiveness, annoyance, tameness, curse-of-knowledge, and pronunciation difficulty.

Required questions:

- What image, emotion, or story appears without a long explanation?
- Does the name offer verbal “legs” for taglines, campaigns, and brand language?
- Which SCRATCH failure is most likely?
- Is any difficulty productive distinctiveness or merely friction?
- Can a normal customer repeat, write, and recommend it?

### `IGOR_DISTINCTIVENESS_LENS`

Apply competitive naming taxonomy, category positioning, emotional engagement, stopping power, and resistance to compromise-driven blandness.

Required questions:

- Where does the candidate sit in the category taxonomy?
- Does it create a distinct naming territory or merely decorate a category cliché?
- Does it provoke useful engagement?
- Are objections evidence of real risk or evidence that the name has character?
- Which candidate is most likely to escape the forgettable middle?

### `ALTMAN_SYSTEMATIC_CREATIVITY_LENS`

Apply systematic creativity, rejection of the single-eureka myth, bad-name and reverse-thinking exercises, broad exploration, and process buy-in.

Required questions:

- Was the candidate discovered through a defensible creative path or lucky attachment?
- What assumptions were inverted to reach it?
- Does it escape over-description?
- Did the team explore enough divergent directions before converging?
- Is enthusiasm based on the name or on the story invented after selection?

### `CATCHWORD_GLOBAL_LENS`

Apply strategy-led creative exploration, memorability, linguistic/cultural review, distinctiveness, practical screening, and careful audience research.

Required questions:

- Does the name balance strategic meaning and creative engagement?
- Can it travel across the defined languages and markets?
- What native-speaker or cultural evidence is still required?
- Is the name distinctive enough for practical trademark pursuit?
- Does audience research test behavior rather than naked preference?

### Lens output

Each methodology lens submits:

```yaml
lens_id:
public_principles_applied:
first_choice:
second_choice:
rejected_candidates:
case_for_first_choice:
strongest_rival_advantage:
strongest_objection_to_first_choice:
rebuttal:
success_thesis:
failure_conditions:
evidence_that_would_change_vote:
initial_vote:
confidence:
source_boundary_confirmed: true
```

### Methodology chamber debate

After blind reviews:

1. pair lenses with the greatest disagreement;
2. require each to restate the opposing case fairly before rebutting it;
3. require each to identify one point conceded to the opponent;
4. resolve factual disputes through research roles, not rhetoric;
5. preserve irreducible differences in weights or risk tolerance;
6. submit final votes only after cross-examination.

A methodology chamber majority has no special authority. Its purpose is to expose different conceptions of what makes a name strong. The controller integrates this chamber with the functional specialist chamber and validated hard gates.

# 7. Generation swarm protocol

The generation swarm occurs before final screening and is separate from the decision council.

## 7.1 Suggested creative subagents

- `MECHANISM_TERRITORY_SCOUT`;
- `RESULT_AND_TRANSFORMATION_SCOUT`;
- `CATEGORY_ESCAPE_SCOUT`;
- `CUSTOMER_LANGUAGE_MINER`;
- `PROPRIETARY_STORY_MINER`;
- `PHONETIC_SYNTHESIZER`;
- `BOLD_AND_CONTRARIAN_EXPLORER`;
- `DOMAIN_LED_CONSTRUCTOR` when domain constraints dominate.

Each receives:

- approved strategic core;
- only relevant territories/word banks;
- phonetic code;
- saturation lists;
- no-go register;
- a method quota;
- a prohibition on trivial variants.

Each returns candidate records, not a persuasive final ranking.

## 7.2 Diversity merge

The controller:

1. normalizes candidates;
2. removes exact and near duplicates;
3. identifies convergent independent ideas;
4. preserves source agent/method metadata;
5. enforces root/suffix/rhythm diversity;
6. rejects prohibited forms;
7. sends only a curated pool to prescreening.

Independent convergence can be a useful signal, but it can also reflect common cultural clichés. Check both interpretations.

# 8. Formal council sequence

## Round 0 — Preflight

The controller verifies:

- brief version;
- candidate IDs and spellings;
- dossier neutrality;
- evidence timestamps;
- role independence;
- missing legal/native/domain evidence;
- valid knockout rules.

If the dossier differs across roles, stop and rebuild it.

## Round 1 — Blind individual reviews

Run specialists independently, ideally in parallel for read-only work.

Each response uses:

```text
ROLE
CANDIDATE RANKING
POSITION ON EACH CANDIDATE
SUCCESS THESIS
EVIDENCE
STRONGEST OBJECTION
FAILURE CONDITIONS
VOTE
CONFIDENCE
MISSING EVIDENCE
```

The controller records the untouched initial vote.

## Round 2 — Disagreement map

Create a matrix:

| Candidate | Support | Opposition | Disputed fact | Disputed weight | Missing evidence | Knockout claim |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Classify disagreement:

- `FACT_CONFLICT`;
- `INTERPRETATION_CONFLICT`;
- `WEIGHT_CONFLICT`;
- `RISK_TOLERANCE_CONFLICT`;
- `BRIEF_AMBIGUITY`;
- `MISSING_EVIDENCE`;
- `IDIOSYNCRATIC_ASSOCIATION`.

## Round 3 — Advocacy assignment

For every viable finalist, assign at least one strongest advocate, even if it is not their personal first choice. The advocate must build the best evidence-based case.

Required `SUCCESS_THESIS`:

```yaml
audience_attention:
processing_and_recall_mechanism:
strategic_meaning:
competitive_separation:
emotional_or_identity_effect:
word_of_mouth_or_search_behavior:
required_descriptor:
required_visual_verbal_activation:
conditions_for_success:
leading_indicators:
falsification_test:
```

This prevents vague claims such as “it sounds premium.”

## Round 4 — Cross-examination

Each advocate must:

1. attack the strongest rival candidate, not the weakest;
2. identify one advantage the rival genuinely has;
3. defend their own candidate against its strongest objection;
4. state what evidence would make them change their mind.

Question types:

- What audience behavior supports this claim?
- Is this association shared or merely personal?
- Does the name work without the story?
- Does the descriptor solve or hide the problem?
- How does it differ from the three closest competitors?
- What is the likely spelling after one hearing?
- What happens when the product expands?
- Which part of the success thesis depends on marketing execution?
- What legal or digital fact remains unresolved?

## Round 5 — Evidence resolution

Research roles answer disputed factual claims. Creative roles may not “win” factual disputes by rhetoric.

Update only:

- evidence status;
- risk classification;
- candidate status;
- unresolved questions.

Preserve original claims and corrections.

## Round 6 — Red-team attack

Red Team receives:

- blind reviews;
- disagreement map;
- advocacy cases;
- updated evidence.

It tests:

- correlated enthusiasm;
- accidental consensus;
- founder attachment;
- missing markets/languages;
- over-reliance on a descriptor;
- legal/domain wishful thinking;
- trendy half-life;
- reputational misuse;
- future-scope failure;
- false precision in scoring.

## Round 7 — Final specialist votes

Each specialist submits:

```text
INITIAL VOTE
FINAL VOTE
CHANGED? WHY?
TOP CANDIDATE
RUNNER-UP
KNOCKOUTS
REMAINING RISK
CONFIDENCE
```

A changed vote is valuable evidence, not inconsistency.

## Round 8 — Controller adjudication

The controller applies this order:

1. validated knockouts;
2. unresolved high-severity evidence gaps;
3. strategic fit;
4. competitive distinction;
5. pronunciation, spelling, and memory;
6. cultural/linguistic fit;
7. digital practicality;
8. preliminary legal perspective;
9. architecture;
10. activation capacity and owner commitment.

The controller must explain:

- why the winner won;
- why the strongest rival lost;
- which dissent remains valid;
- what evidence could change the answer;
- whether the decision is final or pending clearance/test.

# 9. Voting and veto rules

## 9.1 Vote meanings

- `ADVANCE` — candidate is strong enough for the next gate within the role’s mandate.
- `HOLD` — plausible but blocked by a material unanswered question.
- `REJECT` — fails an approved criterion or has a material role-specific flaw.

## 9.2 No simple majority

Seven `ADVANCE` votes do not override one verified direct trademark conflict. Conversely, one creative critic’s dislike does not defeat a strategically and behaviorally strong candidate.

## 9.3 Veto validity

A veto is valid only when it:

- comes from a role with authority over that criterion;
- cites an approved knockout or high-severity risk;
- provides evidence;
- distinguishes fact from interpretation;
- states whether mitigation is possible.

## 9.4 Pareto frontier

Before selecting one winner, identify:

- strategy leader;
- memorability leader;
- differentiation leader;
- practical/domain leader;
- international leader;
- bold leader.

This reveals the true trade-offs and prevents a mediocre average from defeating a strategically superior specialist candidate.

# 10. Scoring discipline

Scores assist comparison; they do not manufacture certainty.

Recommended 100-point matrix:

| Criterion | Weight |
|---|---:|
| Strategic fit | 14 |
| Category distinction | 14 |
| Memorability | 12 |
| Pronunciation | 10 |
| Spelling | 8 |
| Emotional/verbal character | 8 |
| International safety | 8 |
| Domain/digital practicality | 8 |
| Preliminary trademark perspective | 10 |
| Scalability/architecture | 5 |
| Wordmark/verbal-system potential | 3 |
| Total | 100 |

### Rules

- show sub-scores and uncertainty;
- do not average `UNKNOWN` as a neutral middle score;
- use ranges where evidence is weak;
- preserve role-specific scores before aggregate;
- use scores after knockout review;
- do not claim statistical significance.

# 12. Failure and recovery

## Council convergence too early

- randomize candidate order;
- rerun blind reviews in fresh contexts;
- remove founder preference from the dossier;
- appoint a devil’s advocate;
- add an evidence auditor.

## Council cannot distinguish candidates

- the brief may be too vague;
- candidates may be variants of one idea;
- audience/market evidence may be missing;
- scoring may be hiding meaningful trade-offs.

Return to the relevant upstream phase.

## Every specialist rejects every name

Do not choose the least bad. Diagnose whether the failure lies in:

- territories;
- phonetic code;
- domain policy;
- legal crowding;
- architecture;
- generation diversity;
- strategy.

Launch a new methodologically different wave.

## Founder rejects evidence-backed recommendation

Record the preference and reason. A founder’s willingness to champion the name is material, but do not rewrite research to justify the preference. Offer:

- a new brief criterion if the preference reveals a real need;
- a controlled comparison test;
- a documented owner decision against council recommendation.

# 13. Example bounded dispatches

## Strategy Lead dispatch

```text
You are the independent Naming Strategy Lead. Review dossier version X.
Do not inspect other specialist opinions. Evaluate each candidate only against
approved strategic criteria and future scope. Produce the required blind-review
schema. Do not generate replacements, browse, or certify legal availability.
```

## Phonetic Linguist dispatch

```text
You are the independent Phonetic and Sound-Symbolism Linguist. Review the
candidate spellings, target-language rules, and phonetic code. Do not infer
strategy beyond the supplied brief. Provide pronunciations, likely stress,
wrong-hearing tree, sound/fluency hypotheses, competitor phonetic similarities,
and a role-bounded vote. Mark sound symbolism as probabilistic.
```

## Red Team dispatch

```text
You are the Skeptical Red Team. You receive the complete council record after
advocacy. Find hidden assumptions, unsupported claims, correlated enthusiasm,
conflict risks, and ways the recommendation could fail. Attack the strongest
candidate fairly. Do not invent facts; identify missing evidence explicitly.
```

---

## 14. Completion condition

A council is complete only when:

- all required roles produced bounded reports or a degradation is explicit;
- the initial reviews were independent where claimed;
- disagreements were classified;
- every finalist has a success thesis and failure conditions;
- factual disputes were resolved or remain visible;
- red-team findings were adjudicated;
- final votes and changed opinions were recorded;
- knockout rules were applied before aggregate scoring;
- the controller documented the final conditional decision.
