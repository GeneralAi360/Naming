# Sources, provenance, and adaptation boundaries

## 1. Purpose

This skill combines a structured naming workflow with publicly documented professional principles and official screening resources. This file defines adaptation boundaries and what the skill must not claim.

The skill is an original synthesis and orchestration system. It is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or a reproduction of any named practitioner or firm.

---

## 2. Method architecture and adaptation boundaries

The workflow combines briefing, competitive mapping, semantic territories, phonetics, diverse generation, screening and audience validation. Candidate volume is coverage-driven rather than a rigid quota. Scores do not override hard gates, evidence confidence or qualified judgment.

Creative techniques are analytical tools, not a bank of protected names or characters. Methods adapted for this workflow must not be presented as exact proprietary formulations or as endorsements by their practitioners. Separate evergreen brand naming from campaign or trend naming.

Maintain necessary rights and provenance records for any new inputs. Do not copy private briefs, supplied source documents or project histories into the distributable skill. Public methodological citations and license notices remain part of the package where required.

---

## 3. Public professional-principle sources

See [expert principles](expert-principles.md) for the actual lens definitions and current URLs.

The skill uses public principles associated with:

- David Placek and Lexicon Branding;
- Rob Meyerson and How Brands Are Built;
- Alexandra Watkins and Eat My Words;
- Igor International;
- Eli Altman and A Hundred Monkeys;
- Catchword.

Use rules:

- apply only the summarized published principles;
- never impersonate the person or firm;
- never claim their participation, endorsement, private method, or likely personal vote;
- identify the subagent as a `[PRACTICE]_LENS`, not as the actual practitioner;
- cite or link public sources when explaining the lens externally;
- re-check source freshness when material claims are updated.

---

## 4. Official screening sources

The screening protocol starts from current official sources appropriate to the jurisdiction and asset.

As of 2026-09-03, primary starting points include:

- WIPO Global Brand Database;
- EUIPO/TMview and related official EUIPO search interfaces;
- USPTO Trademark Search and official search guidance;
- national/regional intellectual-property office databases;
- ICANN RDAP information and authoritative registry/registrar data;
- official app stores, package registries, social platforms, and company sources.

Coverage changes. The skill must verify current official resources during a live project and record check dates.

---

## 5. Evidence boundary

These are not equivalent:

- source-derived fact;
- current externally verified fact;
- user-reported fact;
- model inference;
- creative interpretation;
- expert-lens judgment;
- legal opinion;
- native-speaker validation;
- audience-research result.

Every material result must identify its class.

---

## 6. Intellectual honesty

The skill must not:

- present a coined name’s story as historical etymology;
- imply that an exact web search proves absence;
- imply that a domain without a site is available;
- imply that one trademark database proves freedom to use;
- claim a person or firm endorsed a name;
- claim a subagent is a real-world expert;
- fabricate respondent, native-speaker, or legal feedback;
- use protected examples as templates for near-copy generation;
- hide uncertainties to make the final shortlist look stronger.

---

## 7. Updating this synthesis

When adding a new methodology or practitioner lens:

1. record the source and date;
2. separate published principles from interpretation;
3. explain what operational behavior changes;
4. identify overlap or conflict with existing methods;
5. add a bounded subagent mandate only when it adds a genuinely different lens;
6. add evaluation cases that prove the new behavior;
7. update `CHANGELOG.md`.
